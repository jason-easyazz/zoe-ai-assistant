Voice turns on 15 of the last 60 days (216 turns), Telegram on 6 days (33), web on 1 (6); 3 LiveKit sessions in 14 days; no Home Assistant voice use. One heavy day (10-12, 94 voice turns), 0 panel turns after 10-16. ## 5. The panel and voice experience (lane I)

- **Daemon:** 4,862 lines, 178 functions, 12 live threads, 716 MB RSS, about 12% CPU idle-listening. Every network call is a bare `requests` call with no session, so each one opens a new TLS connection: about 10 to 15 per 4-second turn for the STT stream plus 17k announcement polls a day. Silero still drops 256 samples per chunk and never resets state (`D:952-975`). `Nice=-5` in a user unit does nothing.
- **Config lives only on the Pi:** 36 of 46 env keys are absent from the repo's example; a second panel built from the repo would wake to "hey jarvis" with the amplitude endpointer and no speaker ID. `hey_zoe.onnx` is not in git, not in any backup, exists in two copies, with no training record. Deploy is hand-run and checks md5; `/health` reports no version; drift is detected only at deploy time. The Pi copy matched main at review time.
- **Latency:** TTFA median 2.33 s, p90 5.64 s (n=150), against 2.48 and 4.79 the week of 09-28: the median improved, the tail got worse, and the tail is the prompt cache (section 0, item 8). Next 500 ms, in order: keep the panel's KV resident (a dedicated llama slot, slot-save, or harness quiet hours); `ZOE_VAD_CLEAN_TAIL_MS=560` then B1.1 speculative start after fixing the Silero feed; STT under speech only partly delivering (12 hits on about 61 turns, miss reason not logged); `ZOE_FIRST_SOUND_CLAUSE`; Kokoro streaming whole units.
- **Wake word:** threshold 0.45 against a dense band of background audio peaking at 0.44 (about 14 near-misses a day, TV hours); 9% of wakes end in the empty-transcript chime; 13 wakes between 01:00 and 05:00 in three months; still no false-accept or false-reject rate. The cheapest instrument: an offline FRR over the corpus's 1.6 s pre-roll plus a 3-second ring buffer saved around each near-miss as a free negative set.
- **Identity:** section 0, item 10. The Resemblyzer shadow would have rejected 33% of owner turns at 0.70; the two speaker models agree on who the owner is for only 56% of corpus clips, and the TV clips' maximum beats the owner's median. The enrolment offer never targets guests, and an unenrolled member is always a guest by voice, so only a PIN step-up can trigger an offer: 4 offer decisions in two weeks, none to an unenrolled member. `child` and `family_member` collapse to the guest matrix.
- **Barge-in** is a hard stop; duck-decide-resume is built, unit-tested and has never run on the panel; 4 of 8 barges since 09-01 were followed by no speech. **Kiosk:** `home.html` is 4,972 lines and 385 KB with 3,872 lines of inline JS and 14 cards; the CSP and PIN-card problems from the 10-04 review are fixed; zero LiveKit token requests between 10-05 and 10-19; about 210 kiosk 403s a day on the actions poll on 10-14 to 10-16, cause not established.
- **Panel hardware:** Pi 5 at 67 °C with `get_throttled=0xe0000` (soft limit and throttling have occurred since boot), no fan detected; Chromium writes about 2.4 GB a day to the SD card (its HTTP cache); the journal is volatile so crash history is lost on reboot; `voice.log` holds transcripts in plaintext.
- **Corpus:** 1,485 unlabelled WAVs, 52% from July, no manifest; the gate pins 20 clips, 14 of which route to the brain; lists (the largest Skybridge domain), music, reminders, notes and money have no transcribed clips; said-vs-did compares each clip with its own baseline, not an expected intent.

## 6. The data model and what a restore recovers (lane J)

- **Postgres:** one `zoe` database (38 MB, 120 tables, Alembic head 0057, one linear chain), `multica`, a leftover `multica_executor_lab`. No `zoe_auth` database: zoe-auth's 16 tables live in `zoe` with no migration owner. 109 of 122 time columns are TEXT in three formats plus 18 epoch floats; one JSONB column in the schema; pgvector available and not installed (embeddings as BYTEA); the app connects as the only role, `zoe`, a superuser, with no statement or idle-transaction timeout; 66 of 72 member-keyed tables have no foreign key to `users`, so every forget path is hand-written.
- **Dead and duplicate shape:** 45 tables with 0 rows (the whole 0052 calendar model, the whole 0053 contacts model, `memory_forgotten` has never held a row), 25 more not written in 30 days; `people` written by 13 modules while the contacts tables stay empty; a reminder exists three times with no link; two music-history tables both stopped in July and August; rooms in three places; 72 of 73 `ui_panel_sessions` rows are not registered panels; 716 `evolution_proposals` pending and 2 approved.
- **The biggest stores of household words are outside Postgres and outside the forget inventory:** the Flue brain's own SQLite (447 MB, 155,121 stream batches, live, append-only, no retention, no forget hook), the root-owned pre-Postgres `data/zoe.db` (454 MB, mounted read-write into zoe-auth), the tracked pre-migration SQLite (section 0, item 1), five stale palace copies of 21k to 30k rows, a July pre-wipe dump, loose dumps under `~/.zoe/backups`. #2110's retention covers two files and prefixed directories; its discovery parses Alembic text and a `user_id` column, so it cannot see zoe-auth tables, `zoe_user_id` keys, or any store outside zoe-data.
- **Chroma:** 307 drawer rows and an audit collection of 19,831 rows holding before-and-after text since July with no age bound; the live collection uses `l2` distance while a fresh palace is created with `cosine`, so a rebuild-from-export restore would re-rank recall; the weekly check never loads the HNSW index; the live backup script lacks the tracked copy's two safety fixes.
- **Restore:** today, nothing off the box survives beyond git, the Pi, the pendant and the cloud accounts. The weekly verify restores into the live instance and counts two tables, inside the night window. The checklist of what #2119 would still miss is in lane J section 6.3. Multi-household: no table carries a tenant key; the household is the string `'family'` in a visibility column plus about 20 singletons; one database and one palace per household is the right shape, not a `household_id` retrofit.

## 7. Test-suite truthfulness (lane K)

- 959 files, 14,721 test functions, 293k lines; 846 files marked `ci_safe`; 104 files (865 tests) never run in the required gate; 10 unmarked `tests/unit` files (143 tests) run nowhere because the nightly is fail-open. Six of the ten largest test files test dev tooling, not the household product (about 16% of the suite).
- **Mock density is healthy at the unit level** (seeded sample of 1,039 functions: 66% real code with real inputs, 28% mock collaborators, 6% mock-only, source-text or cannot-fail). What is untested is the assembled app: 7 files drive the real `main.app`, 74 build their own `FastAPI()`, 115 hand-roll a fake DB, 338 asserts check SQL text, one Postgres file runs in the gate, and the gate runs Python 3.10 while production runs 3.12.
- **Guest identity is checked eight different ways in production** and mocked twenty ways in tests; a `guest-<id>` principal is refused at the memory write wall but passes the read guard (`memory_service.py:1105-1108` vs `user_filters.py:36-47`), latent today because zoe-auth mints a plain `guest`.
- **Contract tests that can pass vacuously** (the `REQUIRED_CHECKS = []` class): `REQUIRED_CONTEXTS` is a hard-coded constant never compared with branch protection; canonical and loader checks match substrings a comment satisfies; the llama-server flag test pins the repo template while the served `ExecStart` comes from an untracked drop-in; `validate_critical_files` checks only existence; the new mutating-route auth test matches by function name and excludes GET, which is where the house-state and settings leaks were.

About 188 tests can be deleted with no property lost; an estimated 300 to 450 source-text tests could become about 50 behavioural ones.

## 8. The iPhone app (lane M), given the owner's decision

- **Distribution:** Apple Developer Program, individual, US$99 a year, plus TestFlight: the family as internal testers (no review), friends' households as external testers by public link (one Beta App Review for the first build), builds expire after 90 days so CI re-uploads one every 60. Ad Hoc is the fallback; the free Personal Team is development-only; Enterprise and Business Manager do not fit a family; Unlisted App Store distribution is the non-expiring exit ramp but needs full review and a demo mode, and Guideline 4.2.3(i) would reject an app that delegates chat to Element X.
- **Stack: native Swift/SwiftUI.** CallKit, PushKit, the Notification Service Extension, PushToTalk, Live Activities, widgets and App Intents all live in native extensions anyway; Omi's own Flutter app replaced its BLE plugin with a 1,848-line native Swift CoreBluetooth manager; Flutter's `dart:io` ignores a user-installed CA on iOS; a webview Element Call conflicts with CallKit's media ownership.
- **Push:** the Jetson can call APNs directly (outbound HTTP/2 to `api.push.apple.com:443` with an ES256 JWT from the `.p8` key; CGNAT is irrelevant); friends' households use a push relay on the411 that holds the key. Send content-free pushes; the NSE decrypts on device; payloads 4 KB (5 KB VoIP). Correction to round 1: Sygnal is AGPL-3.0 since 0.16.0, not Apache-2.0.
- **Calls and intercom:** Plan B rings with a direct PushKit VoIP push (topic `<bundle>.voip`), no special entitlement; iOS 26 terminates an app that fails to report a call, and 26.4 added a `mustReport` delegate, so implement both. Matrix calls ride an alert push through the NSE to `CXProvider.reportNewIncomingVoIPPushPayload` (the Element X pattern, read in code) and need the discretionary `usernotifications.filtering` entitlement. Correction to round 1: Swift-native MatrixRTC exists (element-call-ios on matrix-rust-rtc 0.5.0-rc), behind a developer flag in Element X. Intercom uses the PushToTalk framework (system PTT UI, topic `.voip-ptt`); a Live Activity gives a lock-screen card but does not ring or start audio. VoIP push only for real calls, never for PA or notices.
- **Omi:** the CV1 firmware sets `CONFIG_BT_MAX_CONN=1`, so the panel Pi and the phone are mutually exclusive centrals; the phone is a foreground "sync now" and live companion that releases the link, reusing `POST /api/omi/recordings` with a member-authenticated variant; adopt the MIT Omi Swift SDK pattern, do not fork the Flutter app; since iOS 26 only AccessorySetupKit apps are relaunched after a force-quit.
- **Security:** base URL and CA fingerprint in the panel QR; the app anchors the household CA itself; Secure Enclave device key with signed challenges; the panel advertises an iBeacon the phone monitors as a passive presence hint (a phone cannot be a background beacon); NFC background tag read plus a signed challenge is the cheap Handoff.
- **Sequence and size (estimates):** M0 push, pairing, lists and notices about 3k Swift lines; M1 PTT plus Live Activity about 2k; M2 calls on Plan B about 1.5k plus a one-week ring acceptance test (Matrix adds 4 to 6k and is blocked on the entitlement); M3 pendant about 4 to 5k. Xcode 26 is the upload floor (26.6 needs macOS 26.2+, Xcode 27 needs 26.6+ and Apple silicon); a real iPhone is mandatory; CI on GitHub's macOS runners (free on a public repo) or Xcode Cloud. Android later in native Kotlin at 60 to 80% of the iOS size; PWA plus Telegram until then.

## 9. Systems that do not work together, round 2

1. The test harness, the Samantha bar, the Telegram brain and the panel share one llama slot and one zoe-data worker; the harness is 97% of the load and the household pays in prompt-cache evictions.
2. The deploy workflow restarts the household's voice service on every merged PR; the review pipeline and the household have no shared notion of a quiet hour.
3. The forget cascade and the Flue brain's session store: the brain keeps its own 447 MB transcript history that no forget reaches.
4. Two sources of truth for a conversation (`chat_messages` and the Flue store), for a member (`users` and `auth_users`), for a person (`people` and the empty contacts model), for a room (three places), for a reminder (three rows).
5. The voice gate and the deploy gate: the gate's scope list misses most of the modules the replay imports, and the replay never touches the HTTP route, identity, targeting, TTS or the Pi.
6. The nightly catch-all and its tests: the tests fail closed when node is missing, the workflow fails open.
7. The panel's config and the repo: 36 of 46 keys and the wake model exist only on the Pi.
8. The identity design and the enrolment offer: the only path to being known requires already being known (a PIN).
9. The engineering board on the box and the household's LAN: an engineering tool with a weaker auth posture than the assistant beside it (private note).
10. The public repository and the household: a chat database, coordinates, names and emails have been tracked for months.
